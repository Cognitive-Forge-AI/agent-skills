# Book

Plan for sustained reading and for readers returning to earlier material. Establish the book's promise, audience, and assumed knowledge from the brief. Keep chapter purposes distinct and transitions tied to the reader's progress. Use consistent terminology and references across chapters; explain a concept before later chapters rely on it.

Let subject matter and publishing constraints determine chapter length. A short practical handbook and a narrative book need different pacing. Do not impose universal chapter lengths, promotional openings, exercises, or chapter summaries.

Use cross-references only to real sections or mark a proposed destination as unresolved. Keep supporting notes or appendices available without making readers repeatedly leave the main thread. The chosen structure controls information order, the style controls voice, and presentation controls heading syntax and markup. Preserve deliberate narrative choices when the brief calls for them.
