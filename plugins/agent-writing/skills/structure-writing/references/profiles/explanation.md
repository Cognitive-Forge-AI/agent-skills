# Explanation

Help the reader understand why something works as it does and how ideas relate. Establish the question and the concepts needed to follow the reasoning. Move from familiar context to the mechanism, tradeoffs, or consequences the reader needs to understand.

Distinguish facts, assumptions, and inference. Use examples to illuminate the model when the sources support them; do not create a personal anecdote or claimed result merely to make the explanation engaging. Discuss alternatives only when their differences clarify the topic.

A short explanation may answer one question directly. A longer one can develop a conceptual progression, including limitations and common misconceptions where they matter. Keep operational instructions and exhaustive lookup detail secondary or link to existing material. The explanation profile does not prescribe formality, publication length, headings, or markup; those decisions belong to the other selected axes.
