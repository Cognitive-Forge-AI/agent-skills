# Argument

Present a claim the reader can assess, with reasons and evidence proportionate to its strength. Establish the relevant question and scope. Organize supporting points so the reader can see how each one bears on the conclusion.

Separate a sourced fact from the inference drawn from it. Preserve limitations, uncertainty, and conditions that could change the conclusion. A useful objection or alternative deserves consideration when it affects the decision; do not invent a weak opponent to make the argument easier to win.

Choose the opening and order according to audience knowledge and the brief. A direct claim can lead; sensitive or unfamiliar reasoning may need context first. End where the argument has established its supported conclusion, adding an action only when requested or justified by the brief. Argument is a writing structure beyond the four Diátaxis documentation purposes; it imposes no promotional hook or universal rhetoric.
