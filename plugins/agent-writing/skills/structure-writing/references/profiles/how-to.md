# How-to

Help a reader who already understands the domain complete a specific goal. State the goal and prerequisites that materially affect the procedure. Begin where the reader's task begins; do not add a beginner course as a mandatory introduction.

Order actions by dependencies. Give decision points where the reader's situation changes the path, and keep alternatives tied to a concrete condition. Distinguish prerequisites, optional steps, and required actions. Describe supported success checks so readers can tell whether the goal is complete.

Include failure consequences or recovery guidance when available evidence makes them useful. Do not invent environmental behaviour to fill a procedure. Put extensive theory and exhaustive option descriptions in existing explanation or reference material. A how-to may use numbered steps when presentation permits; its structure concerns the action sequence, while formatting syntax and prose register belong to their own profiles.
