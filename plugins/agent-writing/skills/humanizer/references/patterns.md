# Pattern guide

Use this as a diagnostic catalog, not a blacklist. Edit patterns because they hurt the specific text, not because they appear once.

## High-signal generated-prose clusters

### Empty reframing

Typical shapes:

- "This isn't X. It's Y."
- "It's not just X; it's Y."
- "Less X, more Y."
- "The real question is..."
- "At first glance X, but actually Y."
- rhetorical question followed by its own dramatic answer

Repair: delete the disposable setup and state the useful claim directly. Keep the contrast when both sides carry necessary information.

### Inflated abstraction

Watch for language that sounds important without saying what happened: transformative, groundbreaking, pivotal, comprehensive, seamless, holistic, innovative, game-changing, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, leverage, navigate, landscape, journey, ecosystem.

Repair: state the action, result, constraint, or tradeoff. Keep any term that has a precise domain meaning.

### Ceremonial verbs

Common examples: serves as, stands as, represents, boasts, features, offers, plays a role in, aims to, seeks to, helps to.

Repair: use the direct verb when meaning is unchanged: is, has, uses, gives, shows, causes, changes, adds, removes.

### Dead framing

Common examples: "In today's...", "It is important to note", "It is worth noting", "Let's dive in", "In conclusion", "Overall" as a generic wrap-up, "With that in mind", "That being said", "Moving forward".

Repair: start with the substantive sentence or use a transition that names the real relationship between ideas.

### Assistant residue

Common examples: "Certainly", "Of course", "Happy to help", "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Would you like me to..." when these are not part of the requested artifact.

Repair: remove them from the artifact.

### Mechanical structure

Signals include:

- every paragraph has the same length
- repeated bold-label-colon bullets
- needless headings in short prose
- an intro that previews the piece plus a conclusion that restates it
- constant three-item lists
- every section ending with a tidy moral

Repair: format for the genre. Use prose for connected reasoning and lists for genuine lists.

### Metronome rhythm

Signals include many consecutive sentences with similar length, syntax, and stress pattern, or repeated "X does Y. X does Z. X does W." construction.

Repair: combine or split where the thought naturally calls for it. Do not manufacture random sentence-length variation.

### Decorative analysis

Watch for participial tails such as "highlighting its importance", "underscoring the need", "reflecting broader trends", "paving the way", or "opening the door" when no new analysis follows.

Repair: delete the tail or make the claimed consequence explicit and supportable.

### Forced audience coverage

Typical shapes: "Whether you're a beginner or a seasoned professional..." or broad "from X to Y" ranges whose middle has no meaning.

Repair: name the actual audience or omit the sentence.

## Usually keep

Do not "fix" these merely because they can appear in generated text:

- a single em dash used naturally
- a useful three-item list
- a precise technical adjective
- a necessary "not X, but Y" correction
- a short analogy that genuinely explains a difficult concept
- repeated terminology that is clearer than elegant variation
- formal syntax in a formal document
- polished prose written by a polished human

## Minimal-change test

Before changing a sentence, ask:

1. Is anything unclear, generic, inflated, repetitive, or off-register?
2. Does the change preserve the author's recognizable voice?
3. Is the new sentence better rather than merely less "AI-like"?

If the answers are no, no, or uncertain, keep the original.
